About Us

A community-driven movement for Laguna de Bay

We are a community-driven group dedicated to protecting, preserving, and promoting the sustainable future of Laguna de Bay. We believe that the responsibility of caring for one of the Philippines’ most important natural resources does not rest solely on the government.

It is a shared responsibility that calls for the active participation of communities, businesses, farmers, fisherfolk, organizations, and every individual who benefits from the lake and its resources.

Through information and awareness campaigns, educational initiatives, volunteer activities, environmental cleanups, community programs, and other conservation projects, we aim to inspire people to take meaningful action for Laguna de Bay.

Our goal is not only to protect the lake today, but also to help ensure that Laguna de Bay remains a healthy, productive, and valuable part of our communities for generations to come.

Mission & Vision

Our Mission

To protect, preserve, and promote the sustainable management of Laguna de Bay by empowering communities and encouraging collective environmental responsibility.

We aim to raise awareness about the importance of the lake, promote responsible waste management and sustainable practices, support conservation and rehabilitation activities, and encourage meaningful participation from communities, fisherfolk, farmers, businesses, organizations, and individuals.

Through education, advocacy, volunteerism, community-based projects, and collaboration with stakeholders, we strive to help safeguard the lake’s ecosystems and natural resources.

Our Vision

A clean, healthy, and thriving Laguna de Bay where people and nature coexist in harmony, communities actively participate in environmental conservation, and the lake’s resources are responsibly protected and sustained for present and future generations.

We envision Laguna de Bay as a resilient and ecologically balanced lake—free from preventable pollution, rich in biodiversity, and capable of continuing to support the livelihoods, well-being, and communities that depend on it.

Our Story

From awareness comes responsibility. From responsibility comes action.

Laguna de Bay has long been an important part of the lives and livelihoods of communities surrounding it. It provides resources for fisherfolk and farmers, supports biodiversity, and serves as an important natural resource for the region.

However, as communities, businesses, industries, and agricultural activities expanded around the lake, the pressures placed on its environment also increased.

Over time, different forms of waste began making their way into the lake. Pollutants, together with erosion and sedimentation, contributed to the deterioration of water quality.

The degradation of Laguna de Bay is therefore not simply an environmental problem—it is a community problem. The lake is connected to the daily activities of the people living around its watershed.

The Laguna de Bay Conservation Steward emerges from the belief that protecting Laguna de Bay cannot be the responsibility of one institution alone.

Through environmental education, information campaigns, volunteer activities, clean-up initiatives, community projects, and responsible waste-management advocacy, the organization aims to help people understand the problems facing the lake and the role they can play in addressing them.

The name “Conservation Steward” reflects this purpose. A steward is someone who takes responsibility for caring for something valuable—not because they own it, but because they recognize its importance and understand that it must be protected for others.

The story of Laguna de Bay Conservation Steward is therefore not only a story about a lake affected by pollution. It is a story about people choosing to take responsibility for the future of the lake.

Oplan Estero-Hero

FILTER FIRST. FLOW CLEAN.

“Ang malinis na lawa, nag-uumpisa sa tapat ng ating kalsada.”

Protecting a lake does not always begin at the lake itself. It begins with the water flowing toward it.

Behind our houses, beside our roads, and through our barangays are countless esteros, creeks, drainage channels, and small waterways that carry water through communities.

Oplan Estero-Hero transforms ordinary residents into “Estero-Heroes”—people who take responsibility for the waterways closest to them.

1. Find Your Estero

Identify the creeks, drainage channels, canals, and waterways surrounding your neighborhood. Know where the water comes from, where it flows, and where it eventually goes.

2. Filter First

Prevent waste from entering waterways in the first place. Practice proper waste segregation and disposal at home.

3. Clean the Flow

Organize community-based cleanup activities in appropriate and safe areas. Residents, youth organizations, schools, businesses, and barangay groups can work together to remove accumulated litter.

4. Watch the Water

Be observant. Unusual odors, discoloration, excessive waste, illegal dumping, and other visible environmental problems can be signs that a waterway needs attention.

5. Keep It Clean

A cleanup should not be a one-day activity. The real goal is to develop habits that prevent waterways from becoming polluted again.

Who Can Be an Estero-Hero?

  • Dispose of waste properly.
  • Keep garbage away from drains and waterways.
  • Participate in community cleanups.
  • Encourage neighbors to practice proper waste management.
  • Report illegal dumping and environmental concerns.
  • Support barangay environmental programs.
  • Teach young people to respect local waterways.

Protect Laguna de Bay for present and future generations.

Laguna de Bay is more than the largest lake in the Philippines. It is a source of food, livelihood, biodiversity, and natural resources for countless communities surrounding its waters.


Yet the lake continues to face environmental pressures brought by pollution, improper waste management, urbanization, industrial and agricultural activities, declining water quality, invasive species, and other human and environmental pressures.


These challenges affect not only the lake itself, but also the people and communities whose lives and livelihoods are connected to it.


We believe that protecting Laguna de Bay cannot be the responsibility of the government alone.


It is a shared responsibility among communities, households, businesses, farmers, fisherfolk, schools, organizations, local governments, environmental groups, and every individual who benefits from the lake and its resources.


As The Laguna de Bay Conservation Steward, we are calling for collective action to protect and preserve the lake for present and future generations.
